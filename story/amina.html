FOOD & COMMUNITY · SAMPLE PROFILE

Amina and the first ten orders

A small home-kitchen idea, a simple menu, and ten early orders that helped a young founder learn what customers really needed.

A fictional example created for the StoryCast class project · 5 minute read

01 / THE BEGINNING

Start with what you have

In this fictional profile, Amina loves preparing meals for friends and family. She notices that busy students and workers nearby often need affordable, home-style lunch options. Instead of renting a space or buying expensive equipment, she writes a short menu and asks a few people what they would actually order.

Her first goal is modest: learn whether ten people would pay for a meal made to order. The experiment is small enough to manage alongside her other responsibilities, and it gives her a clear question to answer.

02 / THE CHALLENGE

A good idea still needs a process

The first week is not perfectly smooth. A few orders arrive late, the cost of ingredients is higher than expected, and Amina realizes that preparing every meal at the same time is difficult. These problems are useful information: she needs a clearer order deadline, a simple cost sheet, and a realistic limit on how many meals she can make.

03 / THE LESSON

Listen, adjust, and try again

For the next test, Amina narrows the menu, confirms orders the day before, and tracks ingredient costs. The lesson is not that every idea succeeds immediately. It is that small tests can reveal what to change before a person commits more time or money.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

Three lessons from the story

  • Test a small version of your idea first.
  • Ask customers clear questions and listen to their answers.
  • Track costs and time, not only sales.

LISTEN / READ

The story, in words

This prototype uses a written transcript so the story is accessible without audio. Add a real recorded interview and captions if you produce media.

Read the full transcript

Interviewer: What made you start?

Amina: I noticed people around me needed simple lunch options. I wanted to test whether my cooking could solve a small, everyday problem.

Interviewer: What was the hardest part?

Amina: I underestimated the time and cost. I learned to plan orders earlier and keep the menu manageable.

Interviewer: What advice would you give someone starting?

Amina: Begin with a small test. You do not have to know everything before you ask people what they need.
